\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:dataset}

We use the MAVIC-T 2025 dataset provided by the challenge organizers, which incorporates diverse geospatial data sources including UNICORN, USGS EROS High Resolution Orthoimagery (HRO), and the UMBRA open data program. The dataset consists of spatially aligned 200m$\times$200m image stacks indexed by WGS-84 top-left coordinates, covering multiple geographic regions (UC Davis, Manhattan, Bingham, Centerfield, among others) across three sensing modalities: SAR, EO/RGB, and IR.

The four translation tasks differ in channel configuration and spatial resolution:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{SAR$\rightarrow$EO}: 1-channel $\rightarrow$ 1-channel at $256\times256$;
    \item \textbf{SAR$\rightarrow$RGB}: 1-channel $\rightarrow$ 3-channel at $1024\times1024$;
    \item \textbf{SAR$\rightarrow$IR}: 1-channel $\rightarrow$ 1-channel at $1024\times1024$;
    \item \textbf{RGB$\rightarrow$IR}: 3-channel $\rightarrow$ 1-channel at $1024\times1024$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Dataset Processing Details.}
Each source undergoes preprocessing to ensure georectification and alignment, stored as GeoTIFFs with spatial referencing. For the three $1024\times1024$ tasks, we create crop-augmented training variants by randomly cropping and resizing regions from the original training images. We also apply a quality filter to exclude corrupted, misaligned, or low-quality training samples identified during a data cleaning pass.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:impl_details}

\paragraph{Model Configuration}
Our EarthBridge UNet follows the architecture scaling roadmap of Diffusion Bridge Foundation. For the SAR$\rightarrow$EO task, we use a small config with 64 base channels and channel multipliers $(1,2,3,4)$. For the three $1024\times1024$ resolution tasks, we adopt a medium config with 128 base channels, channel multipliers $(1,1,2,2,4,4)$, 2 residual blocks per level, and self-attention at resolutions $32$, $16$, and $8$. For the concat conditioning mode, the UNet input channels are adjusted based on the source modality (e.g., $1+1=2$ for SAR$\rightarrow$EO, $1+3=4$ for SAR$\rightarrow$RGB). The VP noise schedule uses $\sigma_{\min}=0.002$, $\sigma_{\max}=1.0$, $\sigma_{\text{data}}=0.5$, $\beta_d=2.0$, and $\beta_{\min}=0.1$.

\paragraph{Training}
All models are trained using the Prodigy optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1.0 and a constant learning rate schedule with 500 warmup steps. We use bfloat16 mixed precision throughout. For SAR$\rightarrow$EO, we use a batch size of 48; for the three $1024\times1024$ tasks, we use a batch size of 8 and 512px random cropping. To improve cross-modal feature consistency, we incorporate Representation Alignment (RepA) with $\lambda_{\text{RepA}}=1.0$ and linear decay for the 1024px tasks. EMA with decay 0.9999 is applied. Training runs for 100,000 steps on NVIDIA A100 GPUs using the Hugging Face Accelerate library.

\paragraph{Optimizer and EMA.}
We use the Prodigy optimizer for all experiments, an adaptive learning rate optimizer that automatically tunes the step size, removing the need for extensive search. We set the nominal learning rate to 1.0 and use a constant schedule with 500 warmup steps. Gradient norms are clipped to 1.0, and we use bfloat16 mixed precision. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is applied to model parameters with a decay rate of 0.9999 to improve stability.

\paragraph{Inference and Sampling}
During inference, we adopt the Diffusion Bridge Implicit Model (DBIM) deterministic sampler with 100 steps. For the $1024\times1024$ tasks, we leverage MultiDiffusion for tiled inference with a window size of 512 and stride of 64 to ensure global coherence while maintaining memory efficiency.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation}

We conduct ablation studies on sampling efficiency and training strategies.

\paragraph{Effect of Sampling Steps.}
We evaluate the impact of the number of sampling steps (NFE) on the translation quality using the SAR$\rightarrow$EO task (\cref{tab:inference_speed}). Due to the deterministic DBIM sampling formulation, our model maintains high structural and perceptual fidelity even at low NFE. We observe that 40 steps provide an optimal balance between inference speed and generation quality.
