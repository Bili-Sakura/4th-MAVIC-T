\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

SAR$\rightarrow$EO uses $256{\times}256$ imagery with 89,411 pairs. The other three tasks use $1024{\times}1024$: SAR$\rightarrow$RGB (10,576 orig., 75,703 aug.), SAR$\rightarrow$IR (10,576 orig., 74,317 aug.), RGB$\rightarrow$IR (2,272 orig., 7,388 aug.). For SAR$\rightarrow$IR and SAR$\rightarrow$RGB, we spatially align SAR scenes with optical mosaics before resampling. For $1024{\times}1024$ tasks, we augment training data with sliding-window crops (stride 512).

\subsection{Training Details}
\label{sec:training}

All DBIM models are trained with the Prodigy optimizer~\cite{mishchenkoProdigyExpeditiouslyAdaptive2024}. For $1024{\times}1024$ tasks, we train at $512{\times}512$ and infer at full resolution. The bridge follows a VP log-SNR schedule with $\beta_d=2.0$, $\beta_{\min}=0.1$. We use DBIM for SAR$\rightarrow$EO, SAR$\rightarrow$RGB, and RGB$\rightarrow$IR; CUT for SAR$\rightarrow$IR only. CUT is trained with Prodigy, batch size 2 per GPU across 8 GPUs, for 11 epochs, with NCE temperature 0.1 and $\lambda_{\text{GAN}}=0.5$. Configurations are given in \cref{tab:dbim_config,tab:cut_config}.

\subsection{Hardware and Software}
\label{sec:hardware}

\paragraph{Hardware.}
A single node cluster with 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs (140GB); x86\_64 multi-core CPU (AMD EPYC / Intel Xeon); 512\,GB+ RAM.

\paragraph{Software.}
Python~3.10+; PyTorch~2.8; HuggingFace Diffusers~0.36; Transformers; Accelerate; Rasterio. Code: \href{https://github.com/Bili-Sakura/EarthBridge-Preview}{github.com/Bili-Sakura/EarthBridge-Preview}.

\paragraph{Key Observations.}
\textbf{(1)} SAR$\rightarrow$EO and RGB$\rightarrow$IR are extremely easy to model; we achieve high performance on both test and validation sets, which aligns with peer rankings in previous years. \textbf{(2)} SAR$\rightarrow$RGB and SAR$\rightarrow$IR are quite difficult, also consistent with prior competition results. Given the difficulty of SAR$\rightarrow$IR, we switched from DBIM to the GAN-based CUT. This yielded better results: diffusion bridges for this hard task tend to produce over-smooth, blurry outputs with low training loss that nonetheless deviate from the actual target appearance; CUT's discriminator addresses this. \textbf{(3)} Although inference requires full $1024{\times}1024$ resolution, we find that training our UNet at $512{\times}512$ effectively extends to $1024{\times}1024$ inference without modification. \textbf{(4)} Our solution is computationally heavy: we employ models over 100M parameters and train on a node cluster with 8 NVIDIA H200 GPUs for hours.
