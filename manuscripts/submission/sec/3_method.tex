\section{EarthBridge: Diffusion Bridge Implicit Models for RS Translation}
\label{sec:method}

We present our EarthBridge approach based on \textbf{Diffusion Bridge Implicit Models (DBIM)}~\cite{zhengDiffusionBridgeImplicit2025}, which generalizes the diffusion bridge framework with non-Markovian bridge processes defined on discretized timesteps. Our solution leverages the deterministic sampling property of DBIM to achieve high-fidelity cross-modal translation with significantly fewer sampling steps compared to the vanilla DDBM. \cref{fig:method_overview} shows an overview of our EarthBridge pipeline.

\subsection{Non-Markovian Diffusion Bridges}
\label{sec:dbim_formalism}

In contrast to the continuous-time Markovian bridge processes in DDBM, we consider a family of non-Markovian diffusion bridges defined over a sequence of discretized timesteps $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_N = T$, controlled by a variance parameter $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$. The joint probability distribution $q^{(\rho)}(\bm{z}_{t_{0:N-1}} \mid \bm{x})$ is factorized through a chain of conditional distributions:
\begin{equation}
    q^{(\rho)}(\bm{z}_{t_{0:N-1}} \mid \bm{x}) = q_0(\bm{z}_{t_0}) \prod_{n=1}^{N-1} q^{(\rho)}(\bm{z}_{t_n} \mid \bm{z}_0, \bm{z}_{t_{n+1}}, \bm{x}),
    \label{eq:joint_dist}
\end{equation}
where $q_0$ is the target modality distribution and each step $q^{(\rho)}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{N}\big( a_{t_n} \bm{x} + b_{t_n} \bm{z}_0 + \sqrt{c_{t_n}^2 - \rho_n^2}\, \bm{\eta}_{n+1},\, \rho_n^2 \bm{I} \big),
\end{equation}
with $\bm{\eta}_{n+1} = (\bm{z}_{t_{n+1}} - a_{t_{n+1}} \bm{x} - b_{t_{n+1}} \bm{z}_0) / c_{t_{n+1}}$. Here, $a_t, b_t, c_t$ are known coefficients determined by the VP log-SNR schedule (\cref{sec:background}). Under this construction, the marginal distributions $q^{(\rho)}(\bm{z}_{t_n} \mid \bm{x})$ are preserved to be consistent with the continuous-time forward bridge (\cref{eq:bridge_forward}). The variance parameter $\rho$ controls the level of stochasticity in the sampling process, ranging from a purely Markovian stochastic process to a fully deterministic implicit model.

\subsection{Reverse Generative Process and Sampling}
\label{sec:dbim_sampling}

The generative process is initialized at the source image $\bm{z}_T = \bm{x}$ and iteratively samples $\bm{z}_{t_n}$ from $\bm{z}_{t_{n+1}}$ via a parameterized data predictor $D_\theta(\bm{z}_{t_{n+1}}, t_{n+1}, \bm{x})$. The core updating rule is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \bm{z}_{t_n} &= a_{t_n} \bm{x} + b_{t_n} \hat{\bm{z}}_0 \\
        &\quad + \sqrt{c_{t_n}^2 - \rho_n^2} \hat{\bm{\epsilon}} + \rho_n \bm{\epsilon},
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:dbim_update}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\bm{z}}_0 = D_\theta(\bm{z}_{t_{n+1}}, t_{n+1}, \bm{x})$ is the clean data prediction and $\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}$ is the predicted noise at time $t_{n+1}$. Setting $\rho_n = 0$ for all steps yields the deterministic \emph{Diffusion Bridge Implicit Model (DBIM)}, which can be viewed as an Euler discretization of a probability flow ODE specifically tailored for diffusion bridges.

\paragraph{Booting Noise.} A critical refinement in our sampling procedure is the introduction of \emph{booting noise} at the initial step ($t_N = T$). Since $c_{t_N} = 0$, the denominator in $\hat{\bm{\epsilon}}$ leads to singularity. To address this, we force the initial step to be stochastic by choosing $\rho_{N-1} = c_{t_{N-1}}$, which injects a standard Gaussian booting noise $\bm{\epsilon}_{\text{boot}}$. This booting noise serves as the latent variable, encoding the one-to-many mapping ambiguity in RS translation (e.g., several plausible infrared images for a single SAR input).

\subsection{Architecture and Training Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

\paragraph{UNet Denoising Backbone}
Our denoiser $D_\theta$ is implemented in PyTorch~\cite{paszkePyTorchImperativeStyle2019} and built upon a \texttt{UNet2DModel} backbone from the Hugging Face Diffusers library~\cite{vonplatenDiffusersStateoftheartDiffusion2022} with Adaptive Group Normalization (ADM-style~\cite{dhariwalDiffusionModelsBeat2021}) that accepts timestep embeddings. The network is instantiated with a base channel count of 128 and resolution-dependent channel multipliers---for example, $(1,1,2,2,4,4)$ for $256\times256$ inputs---yielding block output channels of $(128, 128, 256, 256, 512, 512)$. Each resolution level uses 2 residual blocks. Self-attention is applied at spatial resolutions of $32\times32$, $16\times16$, and $8\times8$ via \texttt{AttnDownBlock2D} and \texttt{AttnUpBlock2D} modules, while other levels use plain \texttt{DownBlock2D}/\texttt{UpBlock2D} without attention.

\paragraph{Source Image Conditioning via Channel Concatenation}
To condition on the source image $\bm{x}$, we adopt the channel-concatenation strategy: the pre-conditioned noisy sample $c_{\text{in}} \cdot \bm{z}_t$ and the source image $\bm{x}$ are concatenated along the channel dimension before being fed to the UNet. This doubles the input channels from $C$ to $2C$, where $C$ is the operating channel count of the model (e.g., $C=1$ for SAR$\rightarrow$EO, $C=3$ for RGB$\rightarrow$IR). The UNet output channels remain $C$, producing the raw denoised prediction.

\paragraph{Bridge Scalings and Schedules}
The raw model output $F_\theta$ is post-processed through bridge scalings $(c_{\text{skip}}, c_{\text{out}}, c_{\text{in}})$ derived from the VP log-SNR schedule (\cref{fig:bridge_schedules}). The denoised prediction is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    D_\theta(\bm{z}_t, t, \bm{x}) = c_{\text{out}}(t) \cdot F_\theta(c_{\text{in}}(t) \cdot \bm{z}_t,\, t,\, \bm{x}) + c_{\text{skip}}(t) \cdot \bm{z}_t\,,
    \label{eq:denoiser}
\end{equation}
where the scalings are computed from the VP parameters $\beta_d=2.0$ and $\beta_{\min}=0.1$. The timestep is rescaled as $\hat{t} = 1000 \cdot 0.25 \cdot \log(\sigma + \varepsilon)$ (with $\varepsilon = 10^{-44}$ for numerical stability) before being passed to the model's sinusoidal embedding layer.

\paragraph{Training Objective}
The primary training objective is a weighted mean squared error between the denoised prediction $D_\theta(\bm{z}_t, t, \bm{x})$ and the clean target $\bm{y}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{denoise}} = \mathbb{E}_{(\bm{x},\bm{y}),\, t,\, \epsilon} \left[ w(t) \cdot \| D_\theta(\bm{z}_t, t, \bm{x}) - \bm{y} \|_2^2 \right],
    \label{eq:denoising_loss}
\end{equation}
where $w(t) = A(t) / c_{\text{out}}(t)^2$ is the Karras bridge weighting derived from the combined variance $A(t) = a_t^2 \sigma_{\text{data,end}}^2 + b_t^2 \sigma_{\text{data}}^2 + c^2 c_t$. The diffusion time $t$ (parameterized as sigma $\sigma$) is sampled from a Karras inverse-$\rho$ distribution with $\rho=7$. Detailed hyperparameters are provided in \cref{sec:impl_details}.
